## Context

Software that controls equipment in the field also needs a box to live in, a power supply, and somewhere to mount the network gear. In these projects I took the hardware side as far as accurate CAD, bills of materials and print-ready files. That way it can be built and reviewed with the constraints already worked out.

## What I built

- **Radar retrofit boxes:** pole-mounted connection boxes that sit between a range radar and its site power and network. They let the fleet power-cycle the radar through a relay and report a loss of mains on backup power. There are four variants: supercaps, an ESP32-S3 with a UPS and a network switch, an industrial ESP32 PoE board with a Li-ion UPS, and a DIN-rail carrier for existing connection boxes.
- **Mains circuit boards:** a mains carrier and a relay deck in KiCad, with Gerbers, a BOM and a build package. Scripted checks cover creepage and clearance by net class, board edges, screw heads and the fit of each board in the enclosure CAD.
- **Scripted CAD:** the enclosures are CadQuery models. Revisions come from changing parameters, and the fit checks run again each time. One check keeps the mains terminals at least 15 mm from the ESP32's antenna, even at the worst seating tolerance.
- **Field mounts:** Fusion 360 designs for mounting a satellite internet mast on cart baskets, an outdoor router on small poles, and a tablet on a handle. Each comes with fit checks in Fusion, slicing for ASA, and purchase lists.

## Design decisions

The enclosures follow the same fail-safe rule as the [radar relay firmware](/projects/radar-relay-iot): the relay's normally closed contacts keep the radar powered when the controller is off or missing. The DIN-rail variant keeps all mains wiring on listed DIN parts and metal rail, so no mains voltage touches printed plastic.

Each mount revision is recorded with the field constraint that drove it. Released kits are withdrawn when a constraint changes, rather than being patched in place.

## Where it stands

The retrofit boxes are CAD fit studies. They have not been printed or bench-tested. The mains boards are designed and checked but not ordered, and they stay bench-only until someone qualified has reviewed them. Earlier mount parts have been printed. The hardware for the selected cart-mount design is in hand, and field testing is next. None of this is presented as certified or licensed engineering.
